ECHO ∿ FLUX

the fluid mirror

Two engines fused into one mirror. A real Navier-Stokes fluid simulation runs on your GPU — and your camera feed is shattered into ~65,000 particles that ride that fluid. Stir with a finger and your face swirls away like ink; springs always pull every particle home, so you re-emerge from the chaos. Wave at the camera and the optical flow of your real motion injects force into the water itself — you stir the mirror without touching it.

Nothing is uploaded. The feed exists only as a GPU texture and dies with the tab.

The origin

This is the third act of a challenge that began with one prompt:

"I want to test your capabilities. Create a mobile first webapp that shows what you are capable of and will blow not just my mind but anybody's mind."

That produced FLUX, then PORTAL and ECHO. Then came the fusion request: "Green works the best. Let's create an iteration that combines flux and echo… the face should look like it's drawn from a fluid." Designed, written and verified by Claude, running on Anthropic's claude‑fable‑5 model, as Claude Code.

Under the hood

One self-contained HTML file, zero libraries. The FLUX core: GPU fluid solver (advection, vorticity confinement, Jacobi pressure projection) painting dye, with generative pentatonic audio. The ECHO overlay: particle state in ping-ponged half-float textures, integrated by a shader whose forces are (1) drag-coupling to the fluid velocity field, (2) springs to each particle's home pixel, (3) shimmer. Camera optical flow is injected as force into the fluid velocity field and as faint dye deposits — your real motion literally paints. Particles are colored by sampling the mirrored camera at their home through a per-palette duotone ramp.
